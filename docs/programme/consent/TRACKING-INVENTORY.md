## 3. Verdict — error monitoring (Sentry)

**Error reports stay on without consent; everything else Sentry sends needs it.**

Reasoning:

1. PECR reg. 6 bites on *storing information, or gaining access to information stored, in the terminal equipment*. The Sentry browser SDK as configured sets **no cookie and writes no storage** (prod capture: empty cookie jar and storage on every page). An error report is assembled from the failing script's own state. Whether reading `navigator.userAgent` counts as "access" is contested; the ICO's broad reading says it can. So the verdict must not depend on "no storage" alone.
2. On the purpose test, the two kinds of traffic are different in kind:
   - An **error report** exists only because a fault actually occurred in a page the visitor asked for. Detecting and fixing faults is part of providing the service at all. It is the narrowest thing that can be sent, it is PII-scrubbed, `sendDefaultPii` is off, there is no replay.
   - A **session ping** is sent for *every* visitor on *every* page load whether or not anything went wrong. It measures crash-free-session rate — a usage/health statistic. The service works identically without it. Calling that "strictly necessary" would be labelling for convenience. Same for performance traces.
3. Therefore the line is drawn at the envelope item type, as an **allow-list**: `event`, `attachment`, and user-submitted `feedback` / `user_report` always pass; `session(s)`, `transaction`, `span`, `profile*`, `replay_*`, `client_report` and any type a future SDK adds are dropped without *performance* consent. The gate is at the transport — the one place every envelope passes — so raising a sample rate, enabling an integration or upgrading the SDK cannot bypass it.
4. On external surfaces (customer portal, public quote, worker portal, public enquiry form) performance telemetry is never sent, even for someone who consented elsewhere on the origin. Errors still are.

Legal note — **NEEDS-FOUNDER (legal sign-off)**: the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 amends PECR to add explicit exemptions, including for detecting technical faults and for first-party statistical purposes (the latter with a clear notice and a simple opt-out). I believe those provisions are in force but did **not** verify the commencement position in this session, and this design does **not rely** on them: error reports are justified under the long-standing strictly-necessary test, and statistics are opt-in. If counsel confirms the statistical-purposes exemption applies, the performance category could move to notice-plus-opt-out; that is a business choice, not a compliance need.

Residual privacy points for the privacy-policy workstream (not PECR): Sentry (Germany) must be listed as a sub-processor; error reports transmit the IP address at transport level and breadcrumbs (recent navigation URLs / console) — confirm "store IP" is off in the Sentry project settings (**NEEDS-FOUNDER**, dashboard-only).

## 4. Verdict — first-party performance / analytics beacons

There are none (row 16). Nothing to gate. If one is ever added it is consent-required unless it is genuinely aggregate, cookieless and first-party — and then the DUAA statistical exemption (notice + opt-out) would be the route, after legal sign-off.
